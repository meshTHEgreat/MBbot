Insights — what this means and why

1. The program just did the hardest thing in quantitative research

It found its first statistically defensible effect — family-wise p ≈ 0.024 after simulating its own entire selection process — and then retired the strategy built on it anyway, because two preregistered tests showed the strategy deserved no credit for the effect. Most research programs do the opposite: weak effect, strong attachment. The discipline held exactly when it was most expensive to hold.

2. Why the pipeline null and the incrementality test don't contradict

The pipeline null asks: could random data produce a winner this good through our machinery? Almost never — so something in the data is real. The incrementality test asks a sharper question: does the breakout trigger beat doing nothing-in-particular at the same times, in the same contracts? No. Both can be true when the profitability lives in where and when the strategy trades rather than in why it pulls the trigger. A year of data now says: morning, tight-spread, near-the-money long options drift slightly positive at the midpoint. That is a property of the market's microstructure clock, not a trading strategy — yet.

3. The execution study bought certainty, cheaply

The $5.27 round-trip gap was modeled; now it's measured. Best achievable recovery with preregistered policies: 22.45% — and the tick windows contained zero queue-consistent passive fills, meaning the comfortable story ("just rest at mid and earn the spread") had no factual basis on this book. This number is an asset: every future strategy will be judged against a calibrated execution frontier instead of optimism or pessimism. The reviewer's warning that the study could become a second overfitting layer was neutralized the only way possible — the policy family and stop criteria were hashed 29 minutes before the first tick arrived.

4. What survived, precisely

Three things. The conditions-effect — the weak drift in the morning trade class, now the program's only live hypothesis, and one that requires a forecast layer on better inputs to become a strategy. The instrument — 86.7% of the option grid robust under stressed costs; SPY/QQQ near 97%. And the process — sealed validation and holdout blocks still unspent after four campaigns, a 200+ trial registry, an execution frontier, and a full causal-audit suite. The program's balance sheet is: zero deployable strategies, exceptional infrastructure, one honest lead.

5. What happens next, and what should not

Next: the underlying-data decision (a stock feed pilot for SPY/QQQ — the forecast layer needs real inputs: volume, true ranges, VWAP, realized volatility), and a new preregistered generation targeting the conditions-effect, with controls drawn outside the eligibility windows to avoid circularity. Not next: reopening any retired family with new parameters; reading the sealed blocks; treating 22.45% execution recovery as improvable by wishing; or interpreting the conditions-drift as tradable before it survives its own adversarial test. The registry remembers everything — including that 181 attempts came before whatever passes next.

Bottom line: Campaign 3 is closed with the strongest negative result the program has produced — not "it didn't work," but "here is exactly what worked (the conditions), exactly what didn't (the trigger, the execution rescue), and exactly what it would take to change the answer." That specificity is what four campaigns of discipline were for.
