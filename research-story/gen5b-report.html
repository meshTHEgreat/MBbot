Insights — what this means and why

1. The honest headline, corrected

The first framing of this result emphasized that the economics missed the frozen $2.00 margin by thirteen cents. External review rejected that emphasis, and it was right to: the forecast-information gate failed by twelve dollars, not thirteen cents. A model whose increment lower bound is −$12.24, whose coverage curve is non-monotone, and which loses to "did the price go up recently?" by $20 has not almost-passed anything. The scorecard is retired. The thirteen cents leaves the story.

2. What the guards proved is the durable asset

The replicated class effect survived the two attacks that could have voided it: no lookahead in the liquidity inputs (124,509 rows, zero failures), and the effect grows under executable pricing. Whatever else happens, the program owns one causally-clean, replication-confirmed fact about the market. Its correct classification — per review — is an eligibility filter: a prerequisite for taking a trade, not a reason to take one. A class can beat a worse class while both lose money; this one's own absolute bound still spans zero.

3. The one thread that survives, and why it's allowed to

The trailing-sign rule was registered as a benchmark before its result was known — which is the only reason it may be tested at all. It beat a model that had every advantage. But G5-B is selection evidence for it, not confirmation: the entire lineage — MACD → trend persistence → ORB → condition class → scorecard → trailing-sign — is one adaptive search, and every transition was motivated by the previous failure. So the rule gets exactly one literal, frozen test (G6), preceded by a gauntlet that can kill it cheaply: a standalone economics dossier ("beat the scorecard" could still mean "less negative"), a vehicle comparison (would plain shares beat options for this weak a forecast?), and a power analysis (can 23 sealed sessions even answer the question?). If it fails — the branch closes, by a rule written down before the test.

4. The fragility lesson

Even the positive-everywhere selected subset kept only 21.5% of its midpoint edge after conservative execution, 6.5% under stress. A strategy living in that band is one provider discrepancy or one regime shift from negative. The revised standard replaces the flat $2 margin with three simultaneous criteria — absolute session-level lower bound above zero, a safety reserve above measured execution/provider/operational uncertainty, and a retention floor — because a point estimate that is positive-but-thin is not a margin of safety; it's a rounding error with good posture.

5. Where the program actually stands

Six generations. Established: a reproducible engine, causal cleanliness, an execution frontier, a replicated eligibility regime, and a registry of 200+ retired ideas. Not established: any validated forecast, any absolute profitability, any reason yet to trade. The review's warning is the one to keep: a process can become so good at explaining each failure and generating its successor that it never reaches a terminal decision. G6 exists to be incapable of that — it ends in confirmation or in retired_morning_tight_directional_long_option_branch, with the forbidden-mutations list pre-committed in writing.

Bottom line: replicated tradeability regime ≠ validated forecast ≠ profitable strategy. One exact rule gets one exact test. Then the branch is confirmed or closed — and either outcome will be true, which after 200 retired ideas is the program's entire value.
