Insights — what this means and why

1. What "replicated" actually bought

Every prior positive number in this program came from data that had already been searched. This one didn't: the condition was frozen — formulas, thresholds, matching rules, hashed with a timestamp — and only then were 2023–2025 purchased and opened. The +$2.96 survived contact with two years of unseen market across three regimes, in both symbols, at p ≈ 0.001. That is the difference between "we found something in our data" and "the market does this."

2. The inversion is quiet vindication

Campaign 3's tempting story was "it works on SPY, not QQQ." The matched-baseline analysis called that noise, and the gates refused to let SPY-only ship. On fresh data, QQQ (+$4.45) now leads SPY (+$1.38). Anyone who had followed the seductive path — drop QQQ, trade SPY — would have optimized into the noise and away from the stronger half. The discipline that felt pedantic in Campaign 3 just paid for itself measurably.

3. Relative is not absolute — the distinction that governs everything next

The class beats comparable trades taken elsewhere/elsewhen by $2.96. But its own absolute expectancy at midpoint is +$0.13 with an interval spanning zero — and execution costs sit on top of that. Translation: the market treats these contracts less unfavorably, but "less unfavorable" only becomes an income stream if a forecast layer can select the subset with genuinely favorable movement. That is precisely G5-B's single objective, and why its gates demand absolute conservative profit with a frozen $2/contract margin — not just improvement over the baseline.

4. The factorial's surprise: liquidity is the engine, morning is the seasoning

The liquidity contrast (+$9.63) dwarfs everything. Tight, actively-quoted contracts are simply a different economic environment from wide ones. Three candidate mechanisms — cheaper friction, faster information incorporation, or liquidity as a proxy for favorable underlying states — are now an explicit study item, because a forecast model that secretly rediscovers "spread is narrow" under another feature name would be an expensive tautology. Meanwhile the later×tight cell's strength (+$5.31) seeds the preregistered whole-session challenger — as a new lineage, not a quiet edit to the replicated class.

5. The execution bound moved from "dead" to "unresolved" — handle with care

+$1.01 exists only at the optimistic ceiling — counting fills that public data cannot prove would have happened. The honest statement: public data can no longer refute execution rescue, and only small real orders can settle it. Hence the parallel measurement workstream: shadow logging, broker lifecycle tests, then tiny preregistered live probes measuring fill physics — never conditioned on forecast winners, never confused with strategy deployment.

6. The program's position, stated plainly

After five generations: one replicated relative effect, an execution frontier measured to its public-data limits, a 200+ trial registry, sealed confirmation blocks still unspent, and a forecast study (G5-B) whose foundation guards — lookahead audit on the liquidity inputs, measurement-error reruns — run before any model is fit. Nothing is tradable yet. But for the first time, the program is studying a confirmed property of the market rather than searching for one.

Bottom line: the boxed question is now live — can underlying information identify which eligible morning trades contain enough movement to clear the measured execution frontier? Everything before this was earning the right to ask it.
